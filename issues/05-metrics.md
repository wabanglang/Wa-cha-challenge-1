# [METRICS] Measure dignity, agency, stability, connection, and cost without gaming them

**Owner / originating author:** DOUGLAS W. T., JACKS0N

## Objective

Define a measurement system that rewards improved human conditions—not administrative throughput.

## Proposed metric families

```text
STABILITY := housing continuity | food security | healthcare continuity | document/benefit continuity
AGENCY := goal attainment | participant choice/control | ability to navigate systems without intermediary
CONNECTION := durable trusted ties | reciprocal support | belonging | social isolation reduction
ECONOMIC := earned income | job/education persistence | predictable resources | debt/financial shocks
SAFETY := adverse events | victimization | crisis use | participant-reported safety
FRICTION := time-to-help | repeat forms | failed referrals | travel/wait burden | eligibility dead ends
DIGNITY := participant-reported respect | autonomy | stigma/discrimination | procedural fairness
COST := direct program cost | participant-borne cost | cost per sustained outcome
```

## Measurement rules

- Separate process metrics from outcome metrics.
- Report absolute values and changes, not only percentages.
- Preserve subgroup differences; do not hide inequity inside averages.
- Record missingness and attrition rather than silently dropping them.
- Predefine meaningful-change thresholds where possible.
- Measure adverse effects and participant burden.
- Use the minimum personal data needed.

## Anti-gaming tests

A metric is suspect if improving the number can make participant conditions worse—for example, maximizing referrals by creating unnecessary handoffs or maximizing enrollment while reducing participant choice.

```text
GOODHART-WATCH := metric↑ AND lived-outcome↔/↓ → FLAG
```

## Challenge

Which measures are valid enough to guide a 90-day pilot? Which are too subjective, invasive, manipulable, delayed, or expensive? What should count as a hard failure even if headline outcomes improve?
